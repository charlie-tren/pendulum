Most charts of world politics count countries, which makes Tuvalu equal to India. This one lets you count people instead.

Balance of the coded world

Mean position of governments that have one, from −1 (all left) to +1 (all right). The pale line is the year; the solid line is a three-year trailing average. Countries with no left–right reading are excluded here rather than counted as centre.

Where each figure comes from

No single dataset covers the whole world for the whole period. This shows which source resolved each country-year, so you can see where the numbers are solid and where they are thin.

Ideology crossed with regime type

Left and right tell you what a government wants. They tell you nothing about whether the people under it chose it. Every block below is a share of the group's population, so the area of a block is the answer: how many people live under a right‑wing electoral autocracy, or a left‑wing liberal democracy. Regime classification is V‑Dem's, via Our World in Data.

The same thing over time

Each panel is one regime type. Heights are shares of the whole group, so the four panels together account for everyone — a panel growing means more of the world lives that way.

Reading this honestly

Two kinds of grey
Left–right does not apply is an answer: Saudi Arabia bans political parties, and the Philippines' governing party describes itself as a big tent spanning populism to self-declared socialism. No data is an admission. They are never merged, and neither is ever hidden by rescaling the rest to 100%.
Colours follow the American convention
Blue for left, red for right. Most of the world uses the opposite.
Recent years are provisional
The academic sources end in 2023. Later years lean on Wikidata and hand-coding and are marked as provisional wherever coverage falls below 80%. Treat movement in those years as a gap in the data, not a swing in the world.
A year means 1 January
The party of the chief executive on 1 January, following the Database of Political Institutions. A government that takes office in March counts from the following year.
